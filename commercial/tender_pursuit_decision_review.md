# Tender Pursuit Decision Review

## Purpose

A paid, evidence-backed decision service for an implementation provider deciding whether to pursue a specific tender. The customer is buying a defensible decision before spending bid effort, committing delivery capacity, tying up bid security, or accepting contractual exposure.

This is a decision-support service, not a legal opinion, bid-writing service, bid-submission service, eligibility certification, or guarantee of award.

## Customer and buying moment

**Primary buyer:** owner, CEO, bid head, sales director, or delivery leader at an implementation provider.

**Buying moment:** a specific tender has been identified and the provider must decide whether to allocate resources to it.

The review is not suitable when the provider has not identified a specific tender, cannot provide evidence about its own eligibility/capability, or expects us to guarantee qualification or award.

## Required inputs

1. Complete tender package: notice, original bid, all corrigenda, pre-bid clarifications/minutes, buyer-added ATC, PQC, scope, payment terms, penalties, security/EMD/ePBG terms, experience criteria, resource criteria and every referenced attachment.
2. Provider identity: exact legal bidding entity, group/subsidiary relationships, proposed consortium or subcontracting structure if applicable.
3. Provider evidence: audited financial statements or accepted turnover certificates, contracts/work orders, completion/acceptance certificates, customer references with permission, certifications, staffing evidence, platform/OEM authorisations and current delivery capacity.
4. Decision context: internal bid deadline, available bid team, target geography, delivery capacity, strategic rationale and known exclusions.

Missing inputs are recorded as unknown. We do not fill gaps with marketing claims or assumptions.

## Review method

### 1. Source integrity
- Inventory every tender document and version.
- Record issuing authority, publication/update date, retrieval source and document hash where available.
- Reconcile corrigenda and pre-bid clarifications against superseded text.
- Flag inaccessible, contradictory, illegible or unverified source material.
- Do not issue a final recommendation while material amendments or attachments remain uninspected.

### 2. Requirement extraction
For each material requirement, capture:
- Requirement ID and exact source document/page/clause.
- Requirement text or faithful concise paraphrase.
- Mandatory / scored / optional / conditional status, only where source supports classification.
- Threshold, date, geography, entity and evidence form.
- Consequence of non-compliance where stated.
- Interpretation uncertainty and any clarification needed.

### 3. Provider evidence mapping
Each requirement receives one evidence state:
- **Evidenced:** supplied or independently retrieved evidence directly supports the requirement.
- **Contradicted:** available evidence conflicts with the requirement.
- **Partial:** some but not all elements are supported.
- **Unknown:** evidence is absent or not inspectable.
- **Not applicable:** only when the source and provider structure justify this.

For each item, record the evidence source, date, issuing party, legal entity it relates to, period covered and limitations. Provider website/profile claims remain provider claims unless independently corroborated.

### 4. Exposure analysis
Review, where present in the tender:
- EMD, bid security, performance security and validity/extension exposure.
- Turnover, profitability, net worth and experience thresholds.
- Project-value thresholds, customer type, completion period and reference evidence.
- Scope boundaries, integrations, migration, customisation, hosting, licences and third-party dependencies.
- Staffing, named experts, onsite requirements and replacement obligations.
- Implementation timetable, acceptance tests, service levels and warranty/support.
- Payment milestones, retention, deductions, taxes and payment dependencies.
- Liquidated damages, service credits, indemnities, liability caps and termination.
- Contract duration, extension/option clauses, price variation and change control.
- Consortium, subcontracting, assignment, agency, conflict-of-interest and intermediary restrictions.
- Bid preparation cost, delivery capacity conflict and opportunity cost.

Do not infer legal effect beyond the text. Material legal ambiguity is escalated for qualified legal review.

### 5. Decision
Use one of three outcomes:

**DO NOT PURSUE**
- A mandatory requirement is contradicted or demonstrably unmet; or
- Material exposure is unacceptable to the provider; or
- The provider cannot establish a lawful/permitted bidding structure.

**CONDITIONAL PURSUIT**
- No fatal disqualifier is established, but named evidence, clarification, approvals or capacity conditions must be satisfied before bid submission.
- Each condition has an owner and deadline.

**PURSUIT SUPPORTED**
- All material mandatory requirements are evidenced for the exact bidding entity and structure.
- Material commercial/delivery exposures are understood and accepted by the provider.
- Remaining uncertainties are non-material or explicitly accepted by the decision owner.

This is a decision-support conclusion based on the reviewed evidence at a stated cut-off time. It is not a guarantee that the buyer will accept the bid or that the provider is legally eligible.

If the source package is incomplete, the status is **HOLD — INSUFFICIENT SOURCE PACKAGE**, not one of the three final outcomes.

## Deliverable

A concise decision memorandum with:

1. **Decision at a glance:** outcome, date/time cut-off, tender identity, provider legal entity, decision owner.
2. **Executive rationale:** strongest supporting and opposing evidence.
3. **Tender source register:** complete document inventory, version and retrieval status.
4. **Mandatory qualification matrix:** clause → threshold → provider evidence → evidence state → gap/action.
5. **Delivery and commercial exposure register:** source clause, exposure, potential consequence, owner response.
6. **Clarifications and unresolved questions:** ranked by decision impact and deadline.
7. **Conditions to proceed:** owner, required evidence/approval, due date.
8. **Evidence appendix:** source-linked citations, file/page references and provenance.
9. **Scope limitations:** documents not reviewed, evidence not independently verified, assumptions excluded.

Every conclusion must be traceable to a source. Separate source fact, provider assertion, independently verified fact, inference and unknown.

## Commercial model and engagement controls

- Fixed-fee review, with fee and scope agreed in writing before work starts.
- No default price is set in this document. Existing ₹5,000 versus ₹15,000 pricing conflict remains unresolved.
- Written acceptance must define tender, bidding entity, documents in scope, cut-off date, delivery date, fee, revision boundary and payment terms.
- Do not begin paid work until the fee and scope are accepted and required payment is evidenced.
- No success fee, referral fee, or commission is bundled into this service.
- No bid submission, lobbying, influence, representation to the buyer, legal opinion, technical certification or guarantee of eligibility/award.
- Confidential provider evidence is handled only with explicit permission and disclosed purpose.

## Quality gates

A review cannot be marked complete unless:
- All material tender documents and amendments are inventoried.
- Material clauses are linked to exact sources.
- The exact bidding entity is identified.
- Each mandatory requirement has an evidence state.
- Critical unknowns and contradictions are visible.
- The conclusion follows the stated decision rules.
- A second-pass consistency check confirms that no unsupported eligibility claim appears.

The first commercial proof is one implementation provider that:
1. Identifies a live tender and a real bid/no-bid decision owner.
2. Supplies an authorised tender package and relevant provider evidence.
3. Accepts a written fixed-fee scope.
4. Pays the agreed fee.
5. Receives the decision memorandum.
6. Confirms the review informed a real resource-allocation or bid/no-bid decision.

Until these events occur, the service is a product hypothesis, not validated demand or revenue.
