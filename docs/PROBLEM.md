# The Problem We Are Investigating

Commercial commitments connect work to consequences:

**delivery → acceptance → payment, handover, release, or another obligation**

Yet the evidence needed to establish a commitment's actual state is often spread across contracts, emails, project records, test results, operational systems, acceptance certificates, invoices, and payment records. Different people may hold different parts of the history, use different definitions of completion, or have different authority to recognize a condition.

A status label such as “complete” may therefore fail to answer the questions that matter:

- What exactly had to be true?
- What does the available evidence establish?
- What remains disputed, missing, or unknown?
- Who has authority to recognize the condition?
- Who can take the next action?
- What economic state depends on that action?
- Did that state actually change?

## The problem is reconstruction

The core problem we are investigating is not limited to guarantees or contract administration. It is the difficulty of reconstructing a consequential real-world situation from fragmented, temporally changing evidence well enough to support a decision.

The first commercial research wedge is enterprise software implementation and acceptance, where configuration, testing, operational readiness, acceptance, entitlement, and payment can diverge. This is a validation environment, not a claim that the whole problem is limited to that industry.

## Our working method

For a bounded situation, we reconstruct:

**Required outcome → observed state → evidence-supported gap → unresolved questions → decision and authority → action → verified state change → economic consequence**

We preserve the difference between:
- a source statement and an established fact;
- a party's allegation and a judicial or other authorized finding;
- an event and the interpretation of that event;
- what was knowable at the time and what became clear later;
- a technical milestone and its contractual or economic consequence;
- a plausible intervention and a demonstrated improvement.

Missing evidence remains missing. Conflicting evidence remains visible. A later bad outcome does not, by itself, prove that an earlier intervention was available.

## Current status

This is an active research and commercial-validation effort. The reconstruction method is being tested manually, with software intended to make evidence collection, chronology, comparison, and disciplined reporting more repeatable.

We have published historical research reconstructions based on public records. Those are method illustrations, not proof that the method changes decisions or creates economic value. A suitable current case with an open consequential decision is still needed to test decision impact. Pricing, repeatable acquisition, paid demand, and attributable economic benefit remain unvalidated.

## What would count as progress?

A useful reconstruction should establish what the evidence supports, identify material unknowns, and help an authorized person decide what to do next. We must then test whether that decision changed or was confirmed, whether the resulting state actually changed, and whether any economic benefit can be measured and reasonably attributed.

**Detection is not decision impact. Decision impact is not automatically economic value.**

Our aim is not to replace judgment or promise that disputes can be prevented. It is to make consequential situations more reconstructable and decisions more defensible.
