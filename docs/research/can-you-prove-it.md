# Can You Prove It? — Reconstruction Method

This note describes a research method for examining a consequential commercial commitment using documentary evidence. It is not an offer of services or a legal opinion.

## Questions

1. What obligation or condition was stated?
2. Which sources describe performance, testing, acceptance, or recognition?
3. What does each source establish, and what is only alleged or inferred?
4. What is missing, contradictory, or outside the source record?
5. Which decision or contractual outcome depended on the condition?
6. What outcome can be verified from the available evidence?

## Reconstruction sequence

**Commitment → required condition → observed events → evidence and provenance → recognition authority → decision → outcome**

For each material claim, record the source, date, author or issuing authority when known, relevant passage, and limitations. Preserve the difference between an event and an interpretation of it. Keep later findings separate from what decision-makers could have known at the time.

A condition may be technically achieved without being contractually accepted. Acceptance may occur without payment being made. A legal finding about entitlement does not by itself establish when funds were realized. These are distinct states and should not be collapsed.

## Reporting

A research report should state the scope, source set, chronology, supported findings, unresolved questions, conflicting evidence, and limitations. It should avoid unsupported causal claims. In particular, a later adverse outcome alone does not show that an earlier intervention was feasible or would have changed the result.

## Limits

This method organizes evidence; it does not determine legal rights, replace professional judgment, or establish that a product or intervention is effective. Each published case note applies the method to its cited public record and identifies case-specific limitations.
