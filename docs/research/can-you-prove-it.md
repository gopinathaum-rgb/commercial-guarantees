## Commercial Commitment Audit

A contract can say:

> **"Production deployment will be completed by October 31."**

The useful follow-up question is:

> **What evidence would allow the relevant parties to establish that the commitment was actually satisfied, and what economic state depends on that determination?**

The current research experiment is deliberately small.

Give us **one real commercial commitment** — with sensitive information redacted — and we examine:

1. **Commitment** — What exactly was promised?
2. **Completion condition** — What had to be true for it to count as complete?
3. **Evidence** — What observable facts establish those conditions?
4. **Recognition** — Who or what determines whether the evidence is sufficient?
5. **Economic consequence** — What payment, handover, release, guarantee, obligation, or other consequence depends on the determination?
6. **Reconstruction** — Could an independent reviewer later reconstruct the determination from the available record?

## The economic question

The audit is not primarily about producing a prettier project report.

It asks:

> **What economic transition is waiting on this determination?**

Examples include:

**completion → acceptance**

**acceptance → invoice**

**invoice → payment**

**delivery → handover**

**condition failure → remedy / guarantee / dispute**

The exact consequence depends on the underlying agreement and evidence. We do not assume the answer before reconstructing the situation.

## What this is not

This is not:

- legal advice;
- a contract replacement;
- an automated scoring system;
- a guarantee quote;
- a SaaS product demo.

The first audits are manual research and commercial analysis exercises.

## What we are trying to learn

The central commercial question is:

> **When a consequential commitment is ambiguous or difficult to establish, is there enough economic value in making the relevant state more objectively demonstrable that an organization would use and pay for the method on a real commitment?**

A real artifact is much more informative than a hypothetical conversation.
