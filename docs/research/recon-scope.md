# Recon — Scope and Purpose

## Purpose

Recon reconstructs what is happening in a real-world situation, how its state has changed, what remains unresolved, and what the available evidence supports **before an intervention is chosen**.

Recon is a reconstruction capability, not a product defined by one industry, transaction, or failure mode.

## The reconstruction task

For a bounded situation, the operator works to establish:

1. **Intended or required state** — what needed to become true, based on the relevant requirements, commitments, plans, or operating conditions.
2. **Observed state** — what the available evidence establishes had actually happened at a given point in time.
3. **Change over time** — what changed, when it became observable, and which records support that interpretation.
4. **State gaps and blockers** — where the required and observed states differ, without treating a plausible explanation as a proven cause.
5. **Unresolved matters** — contradictions, missing evidence, competing accounts, and uncertainty that must remain visible.
6. **Evidence-supported conclusions** — what can be said responsibly at that point, and what cannot yet be established.

The reconstruction should be traceable to its sources and reproducible by another operator. It should make uncertainty easier to see, not hide uncertainty behind a confident summary.

## Evidence and time discipline

- Separate what a source records from what an operator concludes.
- Preserve the distinction between fact, allegation, response, procedural event, finding, and unresolved dispute.
- Missing evidence remains missing; it is not proof that an event did not occur.
- A later dispute or failure does not, by itself, prove that an earlier state gap existed.
- Reconstruct each historical point using evidence available by that point. Do not let later realization contaminate an earlier assessment.
- Keep the **evidence/action timeline** distinct from the **realization timeline**. Later outcomes may be compared with earlier reconstruction, but only after the earlier view has been recorded independently.

## Reconstruction is not intervention

Recon's first responsibility is to clarify the situation. Reconstruction does not automatically authorize it to:

- predict an outcome;
- assign a calibrated probability or financial risk score;
- infer intent or responsibility without evidence;
- select or execute an intervention autonomously;
- treat an evidence-supported description as proof that a particular action will work.

A human decision-maker may use the reconstruction to choose what to investigate or do next. Whether Recon improves that decision is a separate question to validate.

## Applications and current research focus

The reconstruction capability may apply to different kinds of operational and commercial situations, including operational recovery, implementation diagnosis, project assurance, and analysis of bounded commercial commitments. These are possible application areas, not a claim that Recon has been validated in each one.

This repository currently uses commercial commitments and enterprise software implementation as a **concrete research and validation environment**. That focus makes the questions testable; it does not define Recon's full scope.

**Recon defines the reconstruction capability. The market determines where that capability becomes economically valuable.**

## What must be validated

Keep three tests separate:

1. **Detection:** Can Recon reconstruct meaningful state changes from evidence?
2. **Lead time:** Can it establish that a material change was observable before formal recognition or later realization, without hindsight?
3. **Economic value:** Does that earlier or clearer understanding change a consequential decision, intervention, or outcome for someone?

Success at one level does not establish success at the next. Historical reconstruction alone does not prove live usefulness, willingness to pay, or commercial value.

## Operating rule

> **Reconstruct first. Preserve uncertainty. Choose intervention second. Validate economic value separately.**

Do not expand the architecture merely because the capability could apply in many domains. Let repeated evidence from real situations show which abstractions and applications deserve to become standard.
