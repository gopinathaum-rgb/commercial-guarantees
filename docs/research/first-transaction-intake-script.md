# First Transaction Intake Script

Use this after a prospect agrees to a short transaction review.

## Opening

“Thanks. We are not trying to audit your whole project.

We want to take one consequential commercial commitment and answer a narrower question: **what would allow both sides to establish, from evidence, that this commitment was actually satisfied?**

We will keep the first pass small and manual.”

## 1. Identify the commitment

Ask:

- What exactly was promised?
- Who owes the commitment to whom?
- Is it contractual, operational, or both?
- What is the relevant deadline or event?

Write the commitment in one sentence.

Do not interpret it yet.

## 2. Identify the consequence

Ask:

- What happens economically if this commitment is established?
- Is a payment released?
- Is an acceptance milestone reached?
- Is retention released?
- Is a guarantee/security consequence triggered?
- Is another contractual right affected?

Record the specific consequence and the amount/currency if known.

## 3. Define the completion condition

Ask:

> “What specifically has to be true for the commitment to count as complete?”

Look for objective conditions, acceptance criteria, test results, deployment state, handover requirements, or other observable conditions.

Separate an operational event from contractual completion.

## 4. Establish the evidence

Ask:

> “If both sides disagreed tomorrow, what records would each side point to?”

Identify the minimum useful evidence.

Examples:

- relevant contract/clause;
- milestone definition;
- acceptance criteria;
- deployment record;
- UAT result;
- production verification;
- ticket/change history;
- correspondence;
- invoice/payment record;
- guarantee/security instrument.

Do not request confidential material until a private channel is established.

## 5. Surface disagreement

Ask:

- Is anyone currently saying the commitment was completed?
- Is anyone saying it was not?
- What evidence does each side rely on?
- Is there an independent verification record?
- Has anything already been adjudicated or settled?

Keep competing claims separate.

## 6. State the bounded question

Convert the discussion into one evidence-testable question.

Example:

> “Can completion of the agreed production-deployment milestone be established by 31 October under the specified acceptance conditions?”

Avoid broad questions such as:

> “Did the vendor successfully complete the project?”

## 7. Close the first review

Explain:

“We will reconstruct only this bounded question. We will distinguish what is established, what is claimed, what is contradicted, and what remains unresolved.

If the evidence is insufficient, we will say so rather than manufacture a conclusion.”

## 8. Commercial value test

After delivering the reconstruction, ask:

1. Did this clarify a decision you actually need to make?
2. Did it expose missing or disputed evidence?
3. Would having this boundary established earlier have economic value?
4. Would you use this method on another commitment?
5. Who inside the organization would own or pay for that capability?

Do not pitch a larger product before these questions are answered.

## Operator discipline

Never turn:

- invoice → entitlement;
- deployment → contractual completion;
- allegation → fact;
- guarantee invocation → proof of underlying breach;
- adjudication → settlement.

The strongest acceptable conclusion may be:

> **Economic state not established from available evidence.**

That is a valid commercial research result.
