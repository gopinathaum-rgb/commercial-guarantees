# Can You Prove It?

## Your contract says the milestone is complete.

**Can you actually prove it?**

Commercial commitments often look objective:

> "Production deployment will be completed by October 31."

But when the milestone has financial consequences, the difficult questions usually come later:

- What exactly counts as completion?
- What evidence proves it?
- Which system or record is authoritative?
- Who decides whether the evidence is sufficient?
- What happens if the customer and provider disagree?
- Can someone reconstruct the decision later?

## We will test one real commitment.

Submit one **redacted commercial commitment** — a milestone, acceptance condition, delivery obligation, go-live requirement, supplier commitment, or similar obligation.

We'll map it through:

**Commitment → Evidence → Verification → Consequence**

and return a short **Commercial Commitment Audit** showing:

- what the commitment actually requires;
- what evidence would establish completion;
- where ambiguity exists;
- who has verification authority;
- what happens if evidence is inconclusive; and
- whether the commitment can be reconstructed after the fact.

### Free while we're researching this problem.

We're investigating whether consequential commercial commitments can be made more objective and easier to verify.

This is research, not legal advice, contract review, or a guarantee that the commitment will be satisfied.
