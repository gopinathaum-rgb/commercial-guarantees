One real commitment. One short audit.

We are researching a simple problem: consequential commitments often have a deadline and a consequence, but the parties may not have agreed clearly enough on what evidence establishes completion.

“Production deployment will be completed by October 31.”

What counts as complete?

Deployment alone? Acceptance tests? Production verification? Customer sign-off?

What evidence proves it?

Which records, timestamps, test results or observations are authoritative?

Who decides?

Can the provider and customer reach the same conclusion from the evidence?

Can it be reconstructed?

Could someone later understand why the commitment was treated as satisfied or not?

The audit

We manually map the commitment through five questions and return a concise written analysis.

01Commitment
02Completion condition
03Evidence
04Verification
05Consequence

The output is a one-page Commercial Commitment Audit identifying what is clear, what is ambiguous, what evidence would be needed, and whether the decision can be reconstructed after the fact.

What can you submit?

One real commitment is enough. It can be old, current, or upcoming.

  • Enterprise software go-live milestone
  • Implementation acceptance condition
  • Supplier delivery milestone
  • Construction milestone
  • Manufacturing acceptance condition
  • Outsourced service commitment
